# Sample Study Material: Introduction to Biology

## Chapter 1: Photosynthesis

### Overview
Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored as glucose. This process is essential for life on Earth as it produces oxygen and organic compounds that serve as food for other organisms.

### Chemical Equation
The overall equation for photosynthesis is:
```
6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 + 6O2
```

### Two Main Stages

#### 1. Light-Dependent Reactions
- **Location**: Thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts
- **Input**: Light, water (H2O)
- **Output**: ATP, NADPH, oxygen (O2)
- **Process**: Light is absorbed by chlorophyll, splitting water molecules and releasing oxygen
- Produces energy carriers (ATP and NADPH) for the Calvin cycle

#### 2. Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent Reactions)
- **Location**: Stroma of chloroplasts
- **Input**: CO2, ATP, NADPH
- **Output**: Glucose (C6H12O6)
- **Key Enzyme**: RuBisCO (most abundant protein on Earth)
- **Process**: Carbon fixation - CO2 is incorporated into organic molecules

### Pigments
- **Chlorophyll a**: Primary pigment, absorbs red and blue light
- **Chlorophyll b**: Accessory pigment, extends the range of light absorption
- **Carotenoids**: Absorb blue-green light, appear yellow/orange

Plants appear green because chlorophyll reflects green light rather than absorbing it.

### Factors Affecting Photosynthesis Rate
1. **Light Intensity**: Increases rate up to a saturation point
2. **CO2 Concentration**: Higher CO2 generally increases rate
3. **Temperature**: Optimal around 25-35°C; enzymes denature at high temperatures
4. **Water Availability**: Essential as a reactant and for maintaining cell structure

## Chapter 2: Cell Division

### Overview
Cell division allows organisms to grow, repair damaged tissue, and reproduce. There are two main types: mitosis and meiosis.

### Mitosis
- **Purpose**: Growth and repair
- **Result**: Two genetically identical diploid daughter cells
- **Phases**: Prophase → Metaphase → Anaphase → Telophase → Cytokinesis

#### Mitotic Phases in Detail
1. **Prophase**: Chromatin condenses into chromosomes, nuclear envelope breaks down
2. **Metaphase**: Chromosomes align at the cell's equator
3. **Anaphase**: Sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles
4. **Telophase**: Nuclear envelopes reform, chromosomes decondense
5. **Cytokinesis**: Cytoplasm divides, creating two cells

### Meiosis
- **Purpose**: Sexual reproduction
- **Result**: Four genetically diverse haploid gametes
- **Divisions**: Two rounds (Meiosis I and Meiosis II)
- **Genetic Variation**: Created by crossing over and independent assortment

#### Key Difference from Mitosis
- Meiosis I: Homologous chromosomes separate (reduction division)
- Meiosis II: Similar to mitosis (sister chromatids separate)
- **Crossing Over** in Prophase I creates genetic diversity

### Cell Cycle
- **Interphase**: G1 (growth) → S (DNA synthesis) → G2 (preparation)
- **Mitotic Phase**: M (mitosis + cytokinesis)

### Cell Cycle Checkpoints
1. **G1 Checkpoint**: Is cell large enough? Nutrients available?
2. **G2 Checkpoint**: Is DNA replicated correctly?
3. **M Checkpoint**: Are chromosomes properly attached to spindle?

## Chapter 3: Genetics and Heredity

### Mendel's Laws

#### Law of Segregation
- Alleles separate during gamete formation
- Each gamete receives one allele from each gene pair
- Offspring receive one allele from each parent

#### Law of Independent Assortment
- Genes on different chromosomes assort independently
- Does not apply to linked genes on the same chromosome

### Genetic Terminology
- **Genotype**: Genetic makeup (e.g., Aa, aa, AA)
- **Phenotype**: Observable characteristics
- **Dominant Allele**: Expressed when present (capital letter, e.g., A)
- **Recessive Allele**: Only expressed when homozygous (lowercase, e.g., a)
- **Homozygous**: Two identical alleles (AA or aa)
- **Heterozygous**: Two different alleles (Aa)

### DNA Structure
- **Double Helix**: Two complementary strands
- **Nucleotides**: Building blocks containing:
  - Sugar (deoxyribose)
  - Phosphate group
  - Nitrogenous base (A, T, G, C)
- **Base Pairing**: A pairs with T, G pairs with C

### Types of Mutations
1. **Point Mutations**: Single nucleotide changes
   - Silent: No effect on amino acid
   - Missense: Different amino acid
   - Nonsense: Creates stop codon

2. **Insertions**: Addition of nucleotides
3. **Deletions**: Removal of nucleotides
4. **Frameshift**: Insertions/deletions not divisible by 3

### Punnett Squares
Used to predict offspring genotype and phenotype ratios.

**Example**: Aa × Aa
```
     A    a
A   AA   Aa
a   Aa   aa
```
Genotype ratio: 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa
Phenotype ratio: 3 dominant : 1 recessive

### Non-Mendelian Genetics
- **Incomplete Dominance**: Heterozygote shows intermediate phenotype (e.g., red × white = pink)
- **Codominance**: Both alleles expressed (e.g., blood type AB)
- **Sex-Linked Traits**: Genes on X or Y chromosomes
  - Examples: Color blindness, hemophilia (X-linked recessive)
  - Males more affected by X-linked recessive traits (XY)

### Polygenic Traits
- Controlled by multiple genes
- Show continuous variation
- Examples: Height, skin color, eye color

## Key Concepts Summary

### Photosynthesis
- Converts light energy to chemical energy
- Two stages: light reactions and Calvin cycle
- Produces glucose and oxygen

### Cell Division
- Mitosis: Growth and repair (2 identical cells)
- Meiosis: Sexual reproduction (4 diverse gametes)
- Crossing over creates genetic variation

### Genetics
- Mendel's laws explain inheritance patterns
- DNA structure determines genetic information
- Mutations can alter genetic sequences
- Non-Mendelian patterns add complexity to inheritance
